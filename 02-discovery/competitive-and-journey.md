# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A young professional in her late 20s who pays for three streaming services herself and cancels and re-subscribes to keep costs down.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** She wants to land on something she’ll enjoy within a few minutes, so every subscription dollar feels worth it.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** After a long day, she opens the app and the homepage pushes new releases she doesn’t care about. She can’t remember which service has the show she wanted, so she bounces between apps without picking anything. Eventually she gives up and opens TikTok, and this service moves to the top of her “cancel next month” list.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** YouTube as her fallback when the app stalls, recommendations from friends she trusts more than the algorithm, and a short list of comfort shows she rewatches instead of looking for something new.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** She asks friends what to watch, because she doesn’t trust the algorithm.
She opens the app and scrolls home rows full of repetitive “Because you watched” picks.
She tries searching by mood (e.g., “slow quiet drama”) and gets irrelevant results, because only exact titles work.
She gives up on discovery and rewatches one of her comfort shows.
If playback freezes on the loading spinner, she closes the app and opens YouTube.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** The moment she has scrolled for 20 minutes past thousands of titles, nothing feels right, and she closes the app without watching anything. She’s paying for the service but getting nothing from it that night.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** “Honestly I just re-watch the same four comfort shows. Discovering anything new feels like a chore, so I don’t.” (UXR-03, Aisha, 27)
